## The deal

12 units, **built 2019**, all 2BR/1BA, 731 SF avg (8,772 SF), **58% occupied**.
Recommended **$900,000 / $75,000 per unit / $103 per SF**; range $855,000 –
$945,000. T-12 income $81,640, opex $64,419 ($5,368/unit), **NOI $17,221**
($13,021 after $350/unit reserves) — a **1.45% in-place cap**. Year-1
underwritten NOI $50,358 ($46,158 after reserves) = 5.13% pro forma cap. Free
and clear; new origination underwritten at $630,000 / 70% LTV / 7.50% / 3-yr IO
/ 360-mo am. IRR 17.93%, 2.22x, 4.26% avg CoC on $276,300 of equity.

## The variant — "lease-up discount," not a distress or renovation discount

The distinguishing fact: **zero bad debt and zero concessions across all twelve
months.** Physical vacancy is the entire problem, on a seven-year-old building
whose in-place rent ($932, $1.28/SF) is already at the comp average ($976,
$1.30/SF). That inverts the usual non-stabilized framing (The Gardens: heavy
economic loss AT high physical occupancy = "a collections and turnover gap, not
a vacancy problem"). Here it is literally a vacancy problem and nothing else, so:

- Label the sale-grid callout **"(19.8% Lease-Up Discount)"**, not "Distress
  Discount" — "the real estate is comparable; only the rent roll is empty."
- Every value-add table in the model is $0. Per the McNeil Star rule, replace
  the expense-comparison table with an **NOI bridge**, and say plainly there is
  nothing to renovate toward on a 2019 asset. The bridge reconciles exactly:
  `13,021 + 9,411 (LTL burnoff 11.2%→5.0%) + 31,043 (vacancy 39.2%→15.0%)
  − 7,317 (expense normalization) = 46,158`. Capitalized at the model's 5.8%
  terminal cap the $33,137 delta is **~$571,000 against a $900,000 basis** —
  "the deal is one lever, not five."

## Two-sided pricing test (Casa Grande method) — the spine of the document

- **vs. the grid:** $75,000 vs. $93,496/unit adjusted = **19.8% discount**.
- **vs. income:** at $900,000 the pro forma cap is 5.13% after reserves (5.60%
  before) against a **5.64% agency-survey average** — a *full* price on income
  the buyer has yet to earn.

Stating both, and reconciling them ("the discount to the grid is exactly the
compensation for carrying the lease-up"), is what makes the number defensible
to an owner in both directions. Two independent cross-checks agreed on the
stabilized value: Year-5 NOI $68,721 at 5.8% = **$98,737/unit**, within 5.6% of
the grid's $93,496.

## Debt capacity — the underwritten LTV was not financeable

Worth running on every non-stabilized deal. The 7.50% coupon gives a 30-yr
constant of **8.39%** (matches the sheet). Sized to 1.25x DSCR:

| NOI basis | Supportable loan | LTV at $900,000 |
|---|---|---|
| T-12 $13,021 | ~$124,000 | 14% |
| Year-1 $46,158, amortizing | ~$440,000 | 49% |
| Year-1 $46,158, interest-only | ~$492,000 | 55% |

The model's own **Year-1 DSCR is 0.98x** (NOI $46,158 vs. $47,250 of IO debt
service; Year-1 cash flow −$1,092, −0.40% CoC). Conclusion for the deck: the
consequence is **equity, not price** — the buyer's real check is ~$413,000, not
the modeled $276,300 — and the recommendation is to pull two or three local
bank / credit-union term sheets before launch. Agency SBL is out until
stabilized (the survey's only sub-50-unit print, a 28-unit 2007 asset in 78233
at $153,571/unit, proves small balance is not the obstacle — unstabilized
income is).

## Data hygiene — reconcile the sheet's own price range before quoting it

**The underwriting summary's "Sales Range" row disagreed with every other row
derived from it.** It printed $860,000 / $900,000 / $950,000, but:

- Price per unit printed $71,250 / $75,000 / $78,750 → ×12 = **$855,000 /
  $900,000 / $945,000** (a clean ±5%).
- Price per SF $97 / $103 / $108 → $855,000 ÷ 8,772 = $97.47; $860,000 gives $98.
- All nine cap-rate cells reconcile to 855/900/945, not to 860/900/950
  (e.g. T-12 1.52% = 13,021 ÷ 855,000; 1.38% = 13,021 ÷ 945,000).

Six of eight derived rows outvote the headline row, so the deck quotes
**$855,000 – $945,000** throughout. Method worth repeating: back-solve the
printed per-unit, per-SF and cap-rate cells for the price they were computed
from, and let the majority win.

Second reconciliation note: page 1's "T-3 cap rate 1.09%" implies a T-3 NOI near
$9,850 while the trending page's third column shows $9,681, and the trending
page's column headers extract out of order. Cite the **published cap rates**
(1.45% / 1.09% / 5.13%) and derive trend statements straight from the monthly
T-12 columns instead — those are unambiguous.

## Yardi mining that paid off (Lackland Terrace, submarket 13)

- **Segment-level insulation, favorable for once.** Blended submarket rent
  growth is +0.6% and the forecast turns negative, but that is a *one-bedroom*
  phenomenon (1BR −4.4% to $799). **Two-bedrooms grew +3.9% to $1,133, 2nd of
  45 submarkets** — and the subject is 100% two-bedroom. Always check the
  unit-type page against the subject's actual mix.
- **Pro forma rent below the submarket segment average** is the strongest
  rent-assumption defense available: $1,050 is 7.3% under the $1,133 2BR
  average. Pair it with the honest caveat that $1.44/SF exceeds the $1.30 comp
  average because the units are small (731 SF) — show the arithmetic, don't
  imply it, and offer a flat-rent sensitivity.
- **Zero-supply submarket**: no completions 2021–2027, nothing under
  construction, and occupancy forecast flat at 90.1–90.3% while rents ease —
  "the trough is a rate story, not a vacancy event."
- **Same-zip pipeline item**: 6802 Marbach Lofts, 78 fully affordable units at
  6832 Marbach Rd, **78227 — the subject's own zip** (NRP Group, submitted
  07/2025, still unapproved), 4.6% of submarket inventory. Disclose it.
- **Transaction page as an independent cross-check**: the submarket has averaged
  $82,000/unit over five years (recommendation sits 8.5% under), and the two
  December 2025 trades bracket it — Big Rock $47K/unit, Hacienda $102K/unit.
- **Occupancy published on two bases** — 85.3% in the submarket report vs. 90.2%
  in the forecast series for the same quarter. Footnote both as published; do
  not reconcile them.

## Disclosures the trailing statement handed over

- **Insurance is $0 in the T-12.** Year-1 adds $650/unit ($7,800) — the model's
  only "value-add" line, and it is *negative* ($135,652 of capitalized value
  destroyed at the terminal cap). Get the declarations page.
- **Taxes reassess DOWN** (rare in Texas, so buyers won't model it): trailing
  $24,458 implies a county assessment near $1,068,000 — *above* the
  recommendation — and a $900,000 sale at the 2026 rate of 2.290% reassesses to
  $20,612.
- **Year-1 expenses are underwritten $7,317 HIGHER than trailing actuals**
  (+$7,800 insurance, +$3,363 management fee, −$3,846 taxes; ties exactly).
  Strongest defensibility line in the package: the pro forma cap is built on
  income normalization, not expense cuts.
- **Utilities $23,421/yr ($1,952/unit) = 28.7% of total T-12 income with zero
  RUBS underwritten.** Present as unbooked buyer optionality (~$172,000 of
  capitalized value), never as NOI.
- Payroll, admin and marketing all print $0 — a lean owner-managed operation.
- **The occupancy trend turned**: vacancy ran ~49% in spring 2025, bottomed at
  25.1% in November 2025, and backed up to **41.7% in March 2026** (worst month
  since July 2025). Note that the Q1 average (36.1%) still beats the T-12
  (39.2%) — state both; a buyer who finds the March backslide unaided prices it
  twice.

## Comp-set handling

- Subject is **the newest asset in the sale grid by 14 years** (comps 1983–2005),
  and the grid already pays for it with +7% to +18% vintage adjustments. Verify
  "newest" against every row — it was *not* true of the rent comps, two of which
  are 2023 vintage.
- **Feature the one true peer by name.** Four One Four (414 Cupples Rd, 8 units,
  2005) traded for exactly $900,000 — $112,500/unit, $100,354 adjusted, still
  above the recommendation. Four of five comps are 104–280 units, so state the
  scale disclosure: a 12-unit asset sells to private local capital writing a
  ~$276K check.
- **Eagle Ridge appears in both the sale grid and the agency survey** with
  different dates (7/28/2025 sale vs. 8/28/2025 origination). Footnote the dual
  appearance and present each as published.
- **Agency comps: lead with the income multiple.** Subject $75,000 ÷ $848/mo =
  **88.4x** vs. the survey average 114.0x — but squarely inside the **84x–96x**
  band of the survey's own value-segment prints (78211 84.3x, 78221 87.0x,
  78240 89.5x, 78238 96.2x). "The per-unit basis is far below the average
  because the income per unit is." Survey High/Low rows are column-wise
  extremes, not transactions — never present them as comps.
