Valuation Strategy

multifamilygrp.com
Aden Crest | Fort Worth, TX

Summary:

Aden Crest is a 202-unit, 1978-vintage garden community in Fort Worth’s Westover Hills submarket. Based on in-place operations, current submarket conditions, and comparable sale and agency-loan data, we recommend positioning the asset at a valuation of $10,100,000 ($50,000/unit), within a defensible range of $9,600,000 to $10,610,000. This is a turnaround and operational-rightsizing story with substantial reversion upside — and the marketing strategy should reflect that reality candidly.

The property is running at a trailing loss (T-12 NOI of approximately –$193,099, a –2.61% in-place cap rate), driven by ~23% economic vacancy inflated by employee-occupied units, a severe outlier expense load, and a softening submarket rent environment. The value proposition to buyers is operational normalization, expense rightsizing, RUBS implementation, and a low going-in basis — not in-place income.

The recommended valuation reflects an approximate 48% discount to the $96,574/unit indicated by adjusted sale comparables — a function of negative in-place cash flow, expensive new-origination debt, and abundant competing distressed inventory across DFW. It is operational and market-driven, not a reflection of the underlying real estate. A $15.0M ask would not generate credible offers and would risk stigmatizing the listing.

Rental Analysis

Westover Hills Market Context
multifamilygrp.com

  • Per Yardi Matrix (June 2026), Westover Hills posted 92.4% occupancy and just 0.8% year-over-year rent growth; average rents ($1,271) are expected to remain essentially flat through year-end 2026.
  • Yardi’s forecast shows submarket rents turning negative through 2027 (–1.4% to –3.9% YoY), not recovering to the prior peak until roughly 2029–2030 — a genuine near-term rent headwind we recommend disclosing proactively.
  • The 333-unit Regency at Ridgmar redevelopment delivers February 2027 (~17.3% of projected submarket inventory growth) — a near-term absorption headwind.
  • Longer-term, this is a mature, value-oriented Renter-by-Necessity submarket with limited forward supply beyond that single project, supporting a recovery thesis for a patient value-add operator past 2029.
  • Subject in-place effective rent of $963/unit sits ~$186 (16%) below the $1,149 comp average — but at $1.39/SF vs. the $1.22 comp average, the gap is an occupancy and collections problem, not a rent-ceiling problem.
  • Loss to lease of 9.1% (~$233,041) burns off as units are renovated and re-leased. Comps support pro forma rents of ~$955–$1,487, requiring ~$7,500/unit of funded interior renovation.

Financing

A Constraint on Pricing, Not a Marketing Advantage
multifamilygrp.com
Aden Crest | Fort Worth, TX

Unlike a below-market assumable loan, financing here is a constraint on pricing rather than a marketing advantage — and we recommend underwriting it honestly. Any buyer must source new-origination debt into an asset with no in-place NOI to service it.

  • With negative trailing NOI, going-in debt service must be funded from reserves and equity until stabilization. Buyers will price this negative-leverage carry directly into their bids.
  • DFW is saturated with lender-owned (REO) and distressed inventory offered directly by lenders, frequently with 0% to 10% down and seller/lender financing — a lower effective cost of capital than a buyer can obtain here, which caps what the market will pay.
  • The projected 24.35% IRR and 2.65x equity multiple are achievable only on successful execution of the operational turnaround and renovation.
  • Given negative in-place leverage and a capital-intensive plan, the buyer pool is a well-capitalized value-add / turnaround operator — not a yield-driven or 1031 buyer seeking day-one cash flow.

Recommendation

multifamilygrp.com
Aden Crest | Fort Worth, TX

We recommend proceeding to market at a valuation of $9,600,000 to $10,610,000 ($47,500 – $52,500/unit) — calibrated to negative in-place cash flow, expensive new-origination debt, soft and declining submarket rents, and heavy competing distressed inventory in DFW. Ownership’s $15.0M expectation is not supportable in the current environment, and pursuing it would deter qualified buyers.

The offering should be centered on three pillars:

1

A low going-in basis far below sale comps and replacement cost

2

Dramatic, buyer-identifiable operational upside from expense normalization and RUBS

3

A long-term submarket recovery thesis for a patient, well-capitalized value-add operator

Key Considerations / Pricing Risk Factors

  • Declining rents through 2027 plus the Regency at Ridgmar delivery — disclose the Yardi forecast proactively to control the narrative.
  • Negative trailing NOI and ~23% economic loss inflated by employee units is the top retrading lever — provide a unit-status roster and collections/occupancy action plan in the DD package.
  • Expensive 8% new-origination debt with no in-place coverage, against cheaper lender-financed alternatives — market to cash-heavy, renovation-capable buyers with realistic expectations.
