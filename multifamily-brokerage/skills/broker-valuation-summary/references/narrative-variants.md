# Narrative Variants — How the Valuation Story Shifts by Deal Hook

Field-tested archetypes from prior builds. Identify which variant the deal
matches BEFORE writing; the pricing story, section structure, table
substitutions, and risk framing all change with it. Property names cite the
build each lesson came from.

## Assumable loan — outsized LTV (leverage is the hook)

When the assumable loan carries a very high LTV (~90%), the pricing story flips
from "distress discount to comps" to "leverage holds pricing near comp value."

Why: Windchase (Jul 2026) — T-12 NOI didn't cover debt service, yet the
recommended valuation sat only ~4% below comp-indicated value because the 89.97%
LTV assumption compressed the equity check to ~13.5% of capitalization and drove
a 20%+ levered IRR. The loan's appeal was leverage, not a below-market rate
(6.46% was near-market).

How to apply: For assumable-loan deals, check whether the hook is (a)
below-market rate, (b) outsized LTV/minimal equity, (c) loan SIZING (see Casa
Grande), or (d) LTV as a PRICE FLOOR (see Las Palmas), and write the Financing
section around the correct one. For stabilization plays, disclose negative carry
explicitly (NOI as % of debt service, Year-1 DSCR/CoC ramp) and target
"regional stabilization operator" buyer profile. Repurpose the
expense-comparison table as a value-add table (driver / cost / NOI impact /
capitalized value) when value creation is income-side rather than opex cuts. If
Yardi shows the subject's own prior trade, use "% below prior basis" as a
pricing defense.

## Assumable loan — below-market rate

(Retreat at Faulkner Point, Jul 2026): when the hook is the rate (3.44% vs.
market), the story is "loan covers debt even at substandard in-place cash flow,"
but pair it with the maturity clock — a ~5-yr maturity into higher rates is the
deal's biggest risk AND the buyer's urgency; frame renovation/stabilization as
needing to complete within the runway. When the subject's own rent roll proves a
renovation premium (premium vs. classic units ≥$100/mo; Faulkner was $250/mo),
flip the template's "don't lead with renovation" default and market it as a
renovation play. If loan balance/LTV is undisclosed by owner, state the
underwritten assumption (e.g., 70% LTV) in the doc and list obtaining the payoff
statement as risk consideration #1. If the brokerage brokered one of the sale
comps, feature it as the most instructive comp and explain the in-place
cash-flow/cap-rate gap plus market-timing difference to bracket the subject's price.

## Free-and-clear / owner-ask-gap

(Hickerson Place, Jul 2026): with no loan to assume, the Financing section's
hook becomes the clean delivery itself (no defeasance/consent, wider buyer pool:
local bank/credit union/cash) plus the underwritten market-rate structure. When
the owner's ask far exceeds comp evidence (Hickerson: $1.5M ask vs. $884.5K
comp-indicated), the Introduction's third paragraph becomes a direct, quantified
rebuttal of the ask (implied cap at the ask, % above comp indication) and the
conclusion adds a "hold and self-stabilize, re-list with 12 months of clean
financials" alternative if ownership won't accept the range. For very small
deals (<20 units), name the buyer-pool constraint explicitly: the operator
capable of the turnaround doesn't buy deals this small, so pricing must meet the
private/local-capital pool — and change the sale-comp callout label from
"Distress Discount" to "At Comp Parity" when the recommendation sits at
comp-indicated value (discount is vs. the ask, not vs. comps). When negative
T-12 NOI is expense-driven (renovation spend run through R&M), the #1
risk/diligence item is the invoice paper trail proving the spend was one-time;
expense normalization (not rent growth) is the value narrative, and the
expense-comparison table stays in its original opex form.

## Underwater / equity-wipeout (HUD-assumption sub-variant)

(The Grove at Killeen, Jul 2026): when market-clearing value sits BELOW the
assumable loan balance, the document's job flips from "how to price it" to
"advise ownership not to sell yet." Structure used:
- Title priceLine and key metrics lead with "Recommended Structure: Loan
  Assumption at Par," not a valuation. Key-metrics table carries THREE values
  side by side: assumption value (= 100% of loan balance), market-clearing value
  range, and comp-indicated value — so the gap is visible on page 1.
- Sale-comp callout gets three rows instead of two: comp-indicated / assumption
  at par / market-clearing at current performance (gold row = the market-clearing
  range, not the recommendation).
- Replace the opex-comparison table with a **DSCR bridge table**: Operating
  Scenario | NOI after reserves | Annual Debt Service | DSCR | Annual Shortfall,
  rows T-12 / T-3 / T-1 / Year 1 stabilized / Year 3 / Year 5, callout row =
  cumulative negative carry + first year of positive coverage. This single table
  carries the whole argument.
- Financing section must state negative leverage explicitly (stabilized NOI ÷
  loan basis vs. loan constant, in bps) — that's why the deal is uneconomic at
  par even after stabilization.
- Frame the par assumption's value as purely defensive and unbookable: avoids
  default/HUD claim, preserves lender relationship and future HUD/agency
  borrowing capacity. Say plainly it "returns no proceeds — it is a debt
  transfer, not a sale."
- Conclusion recommends stabilize-and-hold, priced explicitly: cumulative
  negative carry (e.g. $383K over 4 yrs) vs. certain total equity loss today.
  Use the Yardi forward rent forecast (trough year + cumulative growth to year
  5) as the objective support for waiting.
- If the brokerage has a comparable distressed asset in escrow, that live escrow
  outranks the adjusted comp grid for advising on a sale today — say the grid
  measures real estate, the escrow measures operations. Add a risk bullet that
  the escrow will set the market's anchor the moment it records.
- For HUD loans, risk considerations must include Transfer of Physical Assets
  (TPA) approval timing and HUD-approved-principal requirement, and that the
  lender will likely require a carry/lease-up reserve — which shrinks the buyer
  pool further than the headline equity figure suggests.

## Underwater sub-variant — bridge debt, non-assumable + Section 8/LURA cap-rate penalty

(The Meadows, Aug 2026, 152 units, Garland 75043, 1995 vintage, $17.0M /
$111,842 per unit rec vs. $18.5M bridge originated 12/8/2025; Grove structure
applies with these changes):
- **Non-assumable bridge debt converts "assumption at par" into a
  PAYOFF-SHORTFALL story.** Three-box page-1 bar: comp-indicated / recommended
  valuation / bridge debt payoff (red box). New "Debt Position & Equity Gap"
  section between Introduction and Financing with a payoff-bridge table (gross
  price → 2-3% costs → net proceeds → payoff → CASH REQUIRED FROM OWNERSHIP,
  red callout row). Say plainly "equity is not at risk — equity is gone," and
  quantify the check to close. Sale-comp callout: three rows with the debt/unit
  as a red third row; the debt sitting BETWEEN the recommendation and the grid
  ("financeable asset, unfinanceable operating statement") is the entire lender
  negotiation.
- **Bridge clock replaces the HUD hold-forever option.** Conclusion = three
  named paths (Sell Now / Hold & Stabilize / Negotiated Outcome with the Lender —
  extension w/ paydown, A/B split, DPO, deed transfer), recommendation = engage
  the lender NOW from a prepared position (collections plan, contractor bids,
  tax opinion, this valuation), market after the Yardi trough, with the explicit
  exception "unless the lender forces the timeline." Practical sequencing
  bullet: order payoff statement + extension-test mechanics BEFORE any marketing
  decision — the property won't pass DSCR/debt-yield extension tests on in-place
  income, and the exact shortfall vs. the test is the most useful number in the
  negotiation. Acknowledge in the doc that the advice does not produce a listing
  today — strongest credibility line available.
- **Infer seller basis from the bridge advance** (balance ÷ 70-80% advance rate
  → basis/unit vs. grid and survey) and flag as an inference; pair with the
  origination-date-vs-Yardi-rolldown timeline ("the submarket turned immediately
  after the capitalization was set — a market-timing loss, not an operating
  failure") — that framing is FOR the lender conversation.
- **Section 8 concentration is a cap-rate argument, not just a rent argument.**
  ~30% voucher roll + LURA-era interiors → market prices the asset against 1980s
  workforce comps, not its actual 1990s vintage; say it in the Introduction and
  again under the agency survey. Add a rent-roll composition table (floor plan ×
  conv/voucher/market rent) — voucher rents ABOVE the property's own market
  rents = HAP overhang that buyers underwrite DOWN at turnover; segment it and
  note the pro forma capitalizes zero HAP premium as the credibility point. LURA
  history makes the renovation budget a "catch-up" scope; risk bullet = scope
  uncertainty beyond cosmetics (PCA + third-party bids).
- **When Year-1 UW carries $0 taxes and 0% mgmt fee, quantify the flattery**:
  run the reconciliation (full reassessment + market fee → NOI and cap-rate
  impact) and use it as the arithmetic behind the LOW end of the range. Tax
  consultant opinion = highest-leverage diligence item.
- **Subject NOT insulated from the submarket decline** (in-place rent only ~5%
  below submarket avg): flip the insulation narrative — the T-3
  concessions/bad-debt acceleration IS the submarket decline landing in the
  operating statement; quote the worst collection months. "Anyone selling into
  2026-27 is selling into the bottom of the cycle" (Yardi forecast table as the
  objective basis).
- **In-zip affordable pipeline competes with voucher tenancy** — check Yardi
  development page for planned units in the subject's zip and tie it to the
  vacancy-assumption sensitivity.
- Data hygiene from this build: verify "newest in grid" claims against every
  comp vintage; reconcile the terminal cap to the capitalized values exactly;
  omit source-sheet price/SF ranges that don't reconcile with the $ range; omit
  internally inconsistent loan maturity dates ("5-year term" language instead).

## Loan-SIZING variant / renovation-already-done

(Casa Grande Apartments, Jul 2026, 62 units, Houston 77017, Freddie SBL 6.06%,
83% LTV):
- The hook was neither rate nor LTV but LOAN SIZING. Rate savings vs. a new
  origination were secondary. The decisive math: size a new loan at the market
  coupon (compute the 30-yr constant) and hold it to 1.25x DSCR on T-3
  normalized NOI and on Year-1 pro forma NOI. Show the two resulting loan
  amounts vs. the assumable balance, then convert the gap into the buyer's
  equity check ("the loan roughly halves the check"). That is the lead sentence.
- **Two-sided pricing test.** When comps say cheap but in-place income says
  expensive, say both in the Introduction's third paragraph and reconcile them:
  "$X/unit is a Y% discount to comps, AND $Z is a full price at an A% T-12 cap
  where the market trades at B%." Then bracket with market-cap-rate indications
  on T-3 NOI (low) and Year-1 pro forma NOI (high); the recommendation lands at
  the midpoint, and the assumable loan is what lets a buyer sit at the midpoint
  instead of the floor.
- **Economic-loss bridge table** (5 cols: Value Driver | T-12 Actual |
  Stabilized Target | Annual NOI Impact | Cap. Value @ terminal cap). Rows: mark
  rents to rent-roll market, reduce total economic loss to target %, tax
  reassessment at purchase price × county rate, less mgmt fee on incremental
  revenue. Callout row = T-12 NOI → stabilized NOI + total capitalized value.
  Reconcile the mark-to-market and economic-loss rows so they sum exactly to
  (stabilized GPR × occupancy) − T-12 net rental income; don't apply both at
  full value or you double-count.
- **Convergence exhibit**: capitalize stabilized NOI at the terminal cap and
  compare to the adjusted comp indication. Two independent methods agreeing
  (Casa Grande: within 0.2%) is a strong page.
- **Agency comps: lead with the income multiple, not the cap rate.** Value/unit
  ÷ monthly income/unit. Then concede honestly why part of the gap is earned
  (NOI margin vs. survey-implied) — earned on the operating statement, which a
  buyer can fix, not on the real estate.
- **Renovation already complete** → position as "risk removed, not upside
  remaining"; warn explicitly that any buyer told there's renovation upside will
  retrade when the rent roll shows none. Use $/SF vs. the comp average as the
  proof rents are already maxed.
- **Low median-area-income deals (~$51K)**: compute rent-to-income at in-place
  and at market rent to argue there's no headroom to push rents, and use it to
  argue AGAINST underwriting RUBS — adding billed charges in a high-bad-debt
  submarket trades recoveries for collections. Present RUBS as buyer
  optionality, never as NOI.
- **Audit the T-12 for missing/lumpy months** — often yields the two best
  disclosure items (an inflated single-entry line = unbooked upside; unbilled
  months on a utility = the most likely retrading lever). Always present the
  unfavorable one first and pair it with the favorable one as the offset.
- Assumption approval is risk #1 when T-12 DSCR < 1.0 and Year-1 coverage is
  inside the 1.20–1.25x threshold: servicer will scrutinize sponsor net
  worth/liquidity and may require a carry reserve — recommend pre-qualifying
  buyers on sponsor strength, not price.
- Stress-test the stabilized economic-loss assumption against the Yardi
  occupancy forecast and name the range's low end as the defensible floor under
  that stress. Same for rent growth: show the sensitivity rather than defending
  the assumption.

## Rate-constrained free-and-clear — PREMIUM-to-grid

(Parkwood Apartments, Jul 2026, 28 units, Richardson 75081, $3.0M /
$107,143 per unit):
- When the deal is clean (free and clear, zero bad debt, healthy occupancy) but
  pricing is capped by (a) new-origination rates and (b) a Yardi-forecast
  submarket rent decline, the Introduction's second paragraph names those two
  external constraints explicitly — "the constraint is not the property, it's
  the capital markets and the rent tape" — even when the declines haven't
  touched the subject's cash flow yet.
- **Negative-leverage arithmetic is the pricing-cap argument**: T-12 cap vs.
  note rate vs. loan constant. State that even the pro forma cap sits X bps
  inside the constant — that's why buyers can't pay a lower cap, and why the
  recommendation "already reflects the rate environment rather than leaving
  room to negotiate." Rate widening during marketing = risk #1; mitigant =
  pre-underwritten term sheets from 2–3 lenders before launch.
- **Recommendation ABOVE the adjusted grid is defensible** when free-and-clear +
  clean collections + pro forma yield earn it (Parkwood: 7.0% above). Change the
  sale-comp callout label to "(X% Premium to Grid)" and defend with (1) the
  grid's heavy cap-rate-drift adjustments make it a conservative floor, and (2)
  bracket the other side with the agency survey. "Grid sets the floor, income
  sets the ceiling, recommendation is the midpoint."
- **Insulation narrative for declining submarkets**: when subject in-place rents
  sit far below the submarket average (Parkwood: 27% below), frame the Yardi
  decline as a top-of-market phenomenon that can't reach the subject's price
  point — proof = zero bad debt T-12; recommend packaging 12 months of clean
  ledgers as "the disclosure asset."
- If agency financings in the immediate submarket are ALSO direct rent comps,
  feature them as the two most instructive data points — proves capital still
  transacts in the immediate area at premiums to the ask, so the discount is
  vintage/scale, not location.
- When Year-1 expense underwriting is HIGHER than T-12 actuals, say so as a
  bullet — "the pro forma cap is built on income normalization, not expense
  cuts" is a strong defensibility point.
- Interior renovation dollars underwritten only to support loss-to-lease burnoff
  (no separate rent premium) get a footnoted row in the value-add table and the
  rental section keeps the "don't market as a renovation play" default.

## Rate-constrained free-and-clear — DISCOUNT-to-grid + water-expense theme

(Royal Oaks, Aug 2026, 119 units, Irving 75060, 1963 vintage, $11.0M /
$92,437 per unit, 17.7% DISCOUNT to the grid):
- Parkwood's two-constraint framing applies even when leverage is POSITIVE.
  Positive leverage is the affirmative case FOR the price; the cap-rate LEVEL
  (8.59% where the survey averages 5.74%) is what the rate environment demands.
  Two-sided test per Casa Grande + the income-multiple screen reconciles the
  discount as macro-driven, not property-driven.
- **When the subject's rent sits ABOVE the submarket average, the insulation
  narrative INVERTS into an honest risk** — the Yardi decline CAN reach the
  subject's price point. Let a deliberate Yr2–3 economic-loss ramp carry the
  conservatism argument, and check Yardi's unit-type page for segment-level
  exposure (cite it against yourself before a buyer does).
- **Water-as-theme**: when one utility line is outsized (~21% of in-place NOI,
  zero RUBS recovery), make it a single named theme running through the whole
  doc — simultaneously the biggest expense drag AND the clearest value lever
  (conservation retrofit + RUBS implementation, own capitalized-value table).
  Risk flip side: supply-line-leak scope vs. fixture inefficiency (third-party
  plumbing inspection + bids before launch) and phased RUBS for collections risk.
- **Zero-supply submarket is the lead positive**: no construction and no
  completions in the forecast window → "the forecast trough is not a supply
  event" reframes a rent-forecast decline as a rate correction with a floor.
  Occupancy holding through the forecast = it's a rate story, not a vacancy
  story.
- **Scan Yardi's transaction file for same-zip, same-vintage trades BELOW the
  recommendation** and defend proactively (occupancy, rent level, improvements
  rating) in both the comp section and the risk list rather than waiting for
  diligence to find it.
- Data hygiene: source ranges that don't reconcile to computed values get
  omitted (quote the reconciling mid); agency-survey High/Low rows are
  COLUMN-WISE extremes, not transactions — present as a range with a disclosure
  note, never as individual comps; when the Year-1 expense total excludes
  reserves, label it "operating expenses plus reserves" so it ties to NOI
  exactly; inconsistent term/maturity → "five-year term" language, no date.

## LTV-AS-PRICE-FLOOR variant / down-units stabilization

(Las Palmas, Jul 2026, 182 units, Houston 77039, $13.65M / $75,000 per unit,
$10.0M assumable @ 5.79%, 73.26% LTV):
- **The inversion is the headline insight**: on a loan assumption, a LOWER
  clearing price RAISES LTV. Compute the price at which the fixed balance breaks
  75% and 80% of value and state plainly that below ~80% the servicer won't
  approve the transfer. "The assumable loan is not only the marketing hook — it
  is the floor under the price." Pair with the Casa Grande loan-sizing math so
  both the ceiling and the floor are quantified.
- **Three-source triangulation with a three-row sale-comp callout**: adjusted
  grid / stabilized NOI capitalized at terminal cap / recommendation. Stronger
  than the two-row callout when the recommendation sits ABOVE the grid; label
  the subject row "(X% Premium to Grid)".
- **Rehabilitate a grid dragged down by one outlier**: name the outlier, state
  the ex-outlier average, and show it sits above the recommendation. Then
  concede the top comps' superior location. This defends a premium-to-grid
  recommendation without attacking the grid's method.
- **Down-units deals**: when N units are offline and were scheduled online by a
  date that has passed, say so explicitly and repeatedly — it is the single most
  likely retrading lever. Quantify the buyer's re-scope and recommend
  third-party contractor bids BEFORE launch. Put the down-units line at the top
  of the stabilization bridge with its % of the total bridge so the document
  shows the deal is one lever, not five.
- **Stabilization bridge rows must reconcile to the exact NOI delta.** Fold bad
  debt into the vacancy row rather than carrying a trivial standalone row. Use a
  two-line cell (value + smaller italic $/unit or % of GPR).
- **Unbooked upside as a pricing tool, not a claim**: when the model holds RUBS
  and other income flat while occupancy rises 15+ points, say so as a bullet —
  upside "left for the buyer to find in their own model is worth more to pricing
  than claiming it in ours." Same for a lumpy insurance line (ask for the
  declarations page).
- **A newer asset trading at the subject's per-unit price is a risk bullet, not
  an omission.** Name it, then rebut it (e.g., lender-controlled disposition
  rather than a marketed sale). Always scan Yardi's "Most Recent Sales" page.
- **Yardi submarket report mining** (beyond rent/occupancy): the
  under-construction table often contains a SAME-ZIP delivery landing inside the
  buyer's lease-up window — that specific, dated, in-zip fact is the most
  valuable line in the whole report. Also check whether the subject's OWN prior
  trade appears — it confirms the assumable debt's origin and gives a "% below
  seller's basis" anchor independent of any comp.
- Low-income submarkets: run rent-to-income at in-place, at rent-roll market,
  AND at the Yardi submarket average — if the submarket average lands at ~30% of
  income, the affordability ceiling is the argument for why the pro forma is
  built on occupancy rather than rate. That framing makes an occupancy-driven
  pro forma read as conservative instead of aggressive.

## Tertiary non-stabilized / bank-debt-only free-and-clear

(The Gardens Apartments, Jul 2026, 52 units, Paris TX 75460, $3.0M /
$57,692 per unit, 4.3% premium to grid):
- **Free and clear does NOT mean agency execution.** With ~32% T-12 economic
  loss, Fannie/Freddie is unavailable until stabilization — say so explicitly in
  Financing, then underwrite to local/regional bank debt only (e.g., 75% LTV,
  7.50%, 3-yr IO, 5-yr term) and state that "the valuation must clear that
  buyer's math." The clean-delivery hook still works, just pointed at
  bank/credit-union/cash buyers.
- **Economic-vacancy vs. physical-occupancy gap is the page-one framing** for
  non-stabilized deals: heavy economic loss AT high physical occupancy = "a
  collections and turnover gap, not a vacancy problem." Quote the trough
  collections month and the range; recommend clean ledgers + written collections
  action plan in the DD vault as the retrading defense. In-place DSCR below 1.0x
  on the underwritten IO bank loan is risk #1 — mitigant = indicative term
  sheets from 2–3 local banks + an interest-reserve exhibit.
- **Tertiary buyer-pool constraint + metro REO wave**: when value-add capital
  can buy REO at scale inside the majors, name it as the reason for a targeted
  campaign (regional private operators, 1031 buyers, owner-operators already
  banking locally) over a broad auction, and as a pre-qualification screen.
- **Subject's own prior trade in the comp grid**: when the recommendation sits
  ABOVE the seller's recent basis, disclose proactively and carry the delta with
  the value-add program + free-and-clear delivery "rather than hoping it goes
  unnoticed."
- **A brokerage-brokered stabilized comp in the same town sets the ceiling** —
  use it to argue the model's high end is a post-renovation outcome, not a
  listing strategy. Marketing range = grid-parity low to recommendation.
- **Renovation-play default flipped ON without subject rent-roll proof** when
  (a) a prior program already renovated ~half the units, and (b) the comps
  themselves prove the target rents. Caveat: if pro forma $/SF exceeds the comp
  average, show the arithmetic (smaller units carry higher per-foot rents),
  don't imply it.
- **Flat Yardi forecast reframed as a positive**: ~0% growth with occupancy
  pinned and zero completions → "flat is stable — underwrite durability, not
  growth," contrasted against a declining neighbor county. Pair with the
  affordability-cushion math.
- **No Yardi submarket for the subject's county**: use the nearest tracked
  submarket and disclose the mapping openly — "the data is favorable and
  sophisticated buyers will check it."
- Aggressive underwritten rent growth vs. a flat forecast: present the pro forma
  as comp-anchored (renovated units repricing to what comps already achieve)
  with a flat-growth sensitivity, never defend the trend assumption.
- The lone tertiary print in the agency survey doubles as market confirmation of
  the terminal cap — feature it by name.

## Tertiary sub-variant — EXPIRING agency loan + DISCOUNT-to-grid

(Lofts at Taft, Jul 2026, 35 units, Taft TX 78390, $1.8M / $51,429 per unit,
21% discount to grid; Gardens playbook applies plus the following):
- **An agency loan maturing inside ~13 months is NOT an assumable-loan deal —
  it's a free-and-clear deal with homework.** Say plainly "no buyer assumes into
  an immediate maturity," deliver free and clear at closing, then run the full
  Gardens bank-debt logic. The maturity also does double duty in the Conclusion
  as ownership's own clock: holding means refinancing into the same recourse
  bank debt a buyer would face, while also funding the capex program — the
  strongest "why sell now" available. Risk bullet: confirm prepayment/yield-
  maintenance window vs. expected closing date and order the payoff statement
  early.
- **Recourse-as-screen framing**: state that recourse is why institutional
  value-add capital is absent, and convert that from a weakness into campaign
  design — targeted outreach to recourse-capable regional owner-operators /
  1031 buyers, pre-qualified on willingness to guarantee, not just liquidity.
- **When the recommendation sits BELOW the grid**, keep the two-row "(X%
  Distress Discount)" callout and pair it with the Casa Grande two-sided test —
  reconciled because the buyer carries sub-1x in-place coverage on recourse debt
  through renovation; the discount IS the price of the carry. Locate the
  recommendation between the tertiary ceiling comp and the distress floor comp —
  "exactly where its profile belongs."
- **Really deep in-place coverage deficits get quantified per-year**, and the
  IO-window arithmetic becomes the financing story: pro forma cap vs. IO
  constant works, but stabilized YoC clearing the amortizing constant by only
  ~50 bps — "the IO period is what makes the early cash flow work" is an honest
  bullet buyers respect.
- **A rent comp that is ALSO an agency financing** is the most instructive data
  point — one property proves both the target rents and that capital transacts
  nearby at a multiple of the subject's basis. Scan for this overlap every time.
- **Water/plumbing as a value-add row, not an opex row**: a water bill vastly
  above comps with RUBS recovering only ~half signals supply-line leaks —
  underwrite the repair + low-flow retrofit in the value-add table at 100%+
  return on cost, and add the risk bullet that if the true plumbing scope
  exceeds the underwritten repair, buyers retrade the difference at the terminal
  cap; third-party plumbing inspection + bid before launch.
- **Tax reassessment DOWN is worth a named bullet** when purchase price × county
  rate < trailing taxes — rare in Texas, buyers won't model it unless told.
- **Renovation-play flipped ON purely on comp evidence** (no prior program):
  pro forma rents reset only to what neighboring product already achieves. Show
  the $/SF arithmetic caveat.
- **Declining-submarket insulation, segment-level**: check Yardi's unit-type
  page — citing the subject's own segment (e.g., 1BRs growing while the blended
  submarket declines) beats the blended number.

## Debt-capacity-constrained pricing (price set by the debt, not the real estate)

Use when the deal screens CHEAP on every valuation method (comp grid, income
multiple, cap rate vs. survey) and the binding constraint is financing.

(McNeil Star Apartments, Aug 2026 — 32 units, Dallas 75227, 1985 vintage,
547 SF avg, 84% occupied, $2.4M / $75,000 per unit, 3.2% discount to grid. Deal
spine: *"the price of this asset is set by the debt, not by the real estate."*)

### 1. The debt-capacity test — a named section with its own table
Do not just quote cap rates. Solve for the price a financeable buyer can pay:
- Take the loan constant at the underwritten coupon (compute from first
  principles, 30-yr am — 6.75% → 7.7832%).
- Max debt service = NOI ÷ 1.25. Supportable loan = that ÷ constant. Supportable
  price = loan ÷ LTV.
- Run it on BOTH T-12 in-place NOI and Year-1 underwritten NOI. Those two prices
  bracket the deal; **the midpoint is the recommendation.** (McNeil Star:
  midpoint within 0.04% of the recommendation — that convergence is the
  centerpiece exhibit.)
- Add the inverse statement as a bullet: *"at the recommended valuation a lender
  sizing to trailing income supports only $X — Y% LTV, not 70%. The 70% advance
  rate is available only to a buyer who underwrites the stabilization."*
- The UW sheet's own low-end sensitivity often lands on the in-place
  debt-capacity floor. Say it is a **floor, not a starting point.**

### 2. Rate sensitivity is the risk-#1 table
Hold LTV, DSCR and NOI; vary the coupon ±50 bps and recompute the constant and
supportable price (≈ $4,000–4,400/unit per 50 bps on a small deal). Mitigant is
procedural: pre-underwritten agency SBL + local bank term sheets from 2–3
lenders BEFORE launch. Pair with the negative-leverage line (T-12 cap vs. the
constant, in bps) — that is why buyers cannot pay a lower cap.

### 3. Small average unit size as a structural pricing ceiling
When avg unit SF is far below the comp set:
- The rents are not low, the units are small: rent/unit below comps but $/SF
  above. Best line: *"the only comp achieving a higher per-foot rent than the
  subject is 68% occupied."*
- **In the sale grid, the unit-size adjustment can exceed the NET adjustment.**
  Say plainly: unit size — not condition, location or vintage — is what moves
  the grid.
- Income multiple needs a PEER SUBSET, not the survey average: compare to the
  small/low-income-per-unit prints; that indication is the **ceiling, not the
  target**, because the discount is earned on the operating statement.
- Find the survey's only small-deal print and feature it by name.
- **Scale disclosure**: when no comp is under ~4x the subject's unit count, say
  so — a 32-unit asset sells to equity-constrained private capital writing a
  ~$770K check, not to the buyers of 267-unit assets.

### 4. Assumable loan that adds NO value — the dismissal (inverts house practice)
When the assumable balance is small relative to underwritten value (sub-50%
LTV), the loan is **over-secured and under-levered** and the correct advice is
to market free and clear. Structure as a side-by-side assume-vs-new-origination
table ending in a RECOMMENDATION row, and state explicitly that this departs
from the usual "lead with the below-market coupon" practice and why.
- **The decisive arithmetic is return on the incremental equity**: assuming
  forfeits the proceeds gap; rate savings ÷ extra equity = low single digits.
  No buyer underwriting to 20%+ levered IRR takes that trade.
- Say that DSCR capacity is the scarce resource and the assumption leaves most
  of it unused.
- Second strike: a maturity inside the business plan vs. a new 5-yr term with
  IO. Third: servicer approval + assumption fee + 60–90 days on a pool that
  pays for certainty of close.
- Disclose as optionality for a narrow slice (1031 buyer with excess equity,
  low-leverage/cash buyer) but never build pricing around it.
- **Top-2 risk bullet: order the payoff statement and confirm prepayment /
  yield maintenance.** A below-market coupon with ~3 years left can carry
  material YM.
- The existing loan may not appear in the underwriting sheet at all; caveat that
  remaining amortization/IO status must be confirmed from the loan documents.

### 5. Zero-value-add underwriting → NOI bridge, and the mark-to-market reality check
When every value-add table in the model is blank and mark-to-market is trivial,
say plainly there is nothing to renovate toward and that positioning it as a
value-add deal costs credibility on everything else. Replace the
expense-comparison table with a 4-row NOI bridge (T-12 NOI → GPR/LTL burnoff →
economic loss normalization → expense increase → Year-1 NOI), capitalized at
the terminal cap.
- **Year-1 expenses HIGHER than T-12 actuals is the strongest defensibility
  line** (e.g., an owner-operator management fee normalized upward absorbing
  payroll + marketing = deliberate conservatism).
- **Reframe the economic-loss ask against T-1, not T-12**: the underwriting may
  ask for a few points of improvement from the run rate, not a dozen. Support
  with the loss-to-lease burndown month by month.
- **Unbooked upside**: RUBS held flat while T-3/T-1 annualized run far higher —
  leave it in the buyer's model and say so.
- **Audit for incomplete trailing months**: a month that booked only insurance
  and taxes distorts the in-place cap; annualize the complete months, disclose
  the delta, and show the cap still clears the survey average — that's what
  makes the disclosure safe to lead with.

### 6. Yardi handling
- Occupancy on two conflicting bases (submarket report vs. forecast series):
  **footnote both as published rather than reconciling**; the pricing point is
  the forecast shows no deterioration.
- **Supply CAN be the story**: a single large delivery landing in the same year
  as the forecast rent trough — connect those two facts first, before a buyer
  does.
- **Strongest rent-assumption defense**: a pro forma market rent sitting BELOW
  the submarket's forecast trough average — "the underwriting does not require
  rent growth to work." Offer a flat-rent sensitivity rather than defending
  growth assumptions.
- Segment-level insulation check both ways (dominant unit type vs. its segment
  forecast; minority unit types already pricing above their segment average).
- If the same property appears in both rent and sale comp tables with different
  vintage/SF, footnote the dual appearance and present each as published.

## Renovation-premium evidence rule (cross-variant)

Only market a renovation play when at least one of these holds:
1. The subject's own rent roll proves the premium (renovated vs. classic units
   ≥$100/mo — Faulkner Point was $250/mo);
2. The comps themselves prove the target rents (Gardens, Lofts at Taft); or
3. A prior partial renovation program plus comp evidence (Gardens).
Otherwise keep the template default: do NOT lead with renovation, and if
renovation is already complete, position it as "risk removed, not upside
remaining" (Casa Grande).
